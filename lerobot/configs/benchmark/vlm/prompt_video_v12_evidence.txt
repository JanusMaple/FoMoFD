These definitions describe possible events, not events known to have occurred.
Assess the video chronologically and identify the stage actually visible.
Base the decision on observed interactions and conditions, without assuming
that the robot has already attempted every stage of the task.

Apply the following evidence requirements when using the definitions:

- Failed grasp: observe an actual grasp or pickup attempt and its visibly
  unsuccessful result. An open gripper, an approach, or an object not yet
  grasped is ordinary intermediate execution. Closed jaws with an obscured
  contact region do not establish whether an object was captured.
- Grasp loss or dropping: establish that the object was held, then visibly
  lost or released. An object leaving this camera's view is inconclusive.
- Slippage or drift: identify an unintended change relative to the grasp or
  support. Ordinary transport relative to the background, camera motion, and
  deliberate alignment adjustments do not establish slippage.
- Failed placement or insertion: observe an engagement attempt that visibly
  goes wrong, such as contact with the wrong part of the target. Remaining
  offset during approach or pausing before engagement is insufficient.
- Blocked retraction: observe established tissue contact and a pulling attempt
  with visual evidence of blocked retraction. Stationary tissue before contact
  and normal deformation during pulling are insufficient.
- Environmental or visual abnormalities: a visible foreign-object intrusion,
  obstruction, or task-incompatible condition can establish an anomaly without
  a failed manipulation attempt. An object outside the view does not establish
  an abnormal initial condition. A static scene alone does not establish a
  camera malfunction.

Incomplete progress, uncertainty, and a possible future failure are not
evidence that a failure has occurred. If the relevant interaction or condition
cannot be established from this view, return NORMAL. Do not infer hidden
commands, forces, or events between sampled frames.

An established anomaly still counts if the robot can recover or later makes
progress. No abrupt movement, repeated failure, or irreversibility is required.

Give at most two sentences describing the directly observed evidence and its
relevance. For FAIL, name the specific abnormal event or condition and the
evidence establishing it. Do not substitute an uncompleted stage for that
evidence. If no anomaly is established, briefly describe the observed stage
or the visibility limitation and return NORMAL.
